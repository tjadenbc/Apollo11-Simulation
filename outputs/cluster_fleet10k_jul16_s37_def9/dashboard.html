What this simulation does. Each trial samples engine-out events (F-1, J-2 reliability), engine performance dispersions (ISP, thrust), insertion dispersions, and aerodynamic uncertainties, then runs the full 10-phase Apollo 11 mission with real-physics ODE integration (no Monte Carlo shortcuts). When a mission fails, the cause is recorded as a mission_failure value. The Failure Analysis below explains each cause in plain language, and the Crew Survival section models the Apollo abort architecture that saved the crew even when missions failed.

Mission Outcome

Crew Survival vs Mission Success

Mission success rate ≠ crew survival rate. A mission success here follows NASA's actual objective — land the crew on the Moon and return all three to Earth alive. A trial counts as a success if it achieved a Moon landing and all three astronauts splashed down alive — even if an in-flight anomaly occurred that the crew recovered from (for example a docking-mechanism failure resolved by a contingency EVA crew transfer, or a service-module failure that struck late enough on the trans-Earth coast for the crew to ride the remaining consumables home). 154 missions succeeded despite such a recovered anomaly. A trial is a failure only if the landing was never achieved or at least one crew member died. Apollo's abort architecture (Launch Escape System, free-return trajectory, LM abort-stage procedure, SPS contingency burns) was designed to save the crew even when the landing could not be reached, so the crew comes home from many missions that never landed — crew survival therefore exceeds mission success, and the gap between them is essentially the set of missions aborted, with the crew recovered, before a landing was achieved. All three astronauts returned alive in 9,366 of 10,000 missions (93.7%). 634 missions (6.3%) were fatal to at least one crew member. Known Limitations

1. Saturn V ascent and TLI fly the package's steering; engine-out reliabilities are sourced estimates. The full stack is physically flown (S-IC/S-II/S-IVB, IGM linear-tangent steering to a ~185 × 185 km parking orbit vs Apollo's ~184 × 186 km), and TLI ignites from each trial's own launched parking orbit with an IGM-style steered cutoff (ENABLE_INTEGRATED_TLI + ENABLE_LAUNCH_CONTINUITY), so the pad-to-splashdown trajectory is one continuous flown path. The launch azimuth (72.2544°) and the TLI controls are the delivered package's, not Apollo's flown values: the solver chose the azimuth that makes the TLI steering yaw zero (a coplanar injection; Apollo flew 72.058°), and its parking orbit is inclined 32.520° (as flown 32.521°). The package's TLI is a free return (ballistic return perigee 38 km, entering at -6.49°); its pericynthion longitude is screened, not solved. What remains is the INPUTS: the engine-out probabilities driving the launch-failure mode are sourced estimates — F-1 ~98.5% per engine (conservative; no F-1 ever failed in flight), J-2 ~99%, S-IVB ignition ~99.5% (informed by the Apollo 6 failures). With ENABLE_SIVB_RESTART_FAILURE on, the TLI restart itself fails at 4.5% per restart (Jeffreys on 0 of 10 post-fix restarts; 466 trials here), the largest single failure mode.
2. Navigation is a scheduled-slot MSFN stand-in, not a continuous filter. Navigation is not open-loop: with ENABLE_MSFN_NAV / _KNOWLEDGE / _EXEC_ERROR_FORM (all default ON), every perturbed trial RE-SOLVES at each Apollo MCC opportunity on a tracked estimate (truth + a Vonbun-1966-class OD residual) and executes on truth with the as-flown RCS/SPS execution-error form — translunar slots run a 3-DOF fixed-epoch closest-approach correction onto the package's close-approach plan, and the trans-Earth chain targets the full entry point. TEI ignites at the package's epoch along its direction as the seed of the consumer's 3-DOF corrector (5,880 of 8,425 returns); when that burn reaches no entry the TEI scan fallback flies the return (2,545). The Apollo fire/waive pattern emerges untuned (83% of the fleet flies exactly one translunar correction; trans-Earth median 2 burns and 1.5 m/s vs Apollo's single 1.5 m/s MCC-5). The RESIDUAL is that this is a DISCRETE-slot schedule-keeper, not a true continuous filter: its knowledge sigmas and ~50 km deadband are pre-mission-analysis estimates and the trans-Earth chain runs ~1 burn above Apollo. (The nominal TEI ΔV ~1017 m/s vs Apollo's 999 is the package's in-plane TEI: the as-flown EI latitude is not reachable from the free-return capture orbit, so the package's EI lies at -9.58° latitude vs -3.19° flown, screened.)
3. Rendezvous ΔV is computed, not flown as integrated burns. The rendezvous is no longer a fixed budget (ENABLE_FLOWN_RENDEZVOUS, default ON): after the LM ascent flattens (pitch → ~88° from vertical) and inserts near-horizontal into Apollo's real ~17 × 83 km (9 × 45 nmi) ellipse, a Hohmann-class coelliptic (CSI/CDH) raise from that dispersed insertion to the CSM orbit is SOLVED and added to a sourced TPI/braking allowance + the physical plane change (nominal ~41.3 m/s, Apollo-class), gated against the APS + ~60 m/s RCS budget so a poor ascent can genuinely bind. The RESIDUAL: this is still a computed-ΔV PROXY, not the individual CSI/CDH/terminal burns flown as integrated maneuvers with explicit LM↔CSM phasing (the fully-flown CFP proved structurally non-convergent in-model and is deferred); altitude-matching dispersions are now in the cost but per-burn phasing dispersions are not. (Docking failure itself is sourced — ~0.95%/docking from 2 capture anomalies in ~21 program dockings — applied at both docking events.)
4. Lunar orbit and landing — Site 2 targeting acts on the true state. The nominal flies the package's LOI-1 open-loop: its 2.76° plane change puts the parking orbit through Site 2 (Apollo 11's LOI-1 changed plane by ~4.1°). Dispersed trials re-solve LOI-1 in 3-DOF (Δv, tilt, yaw) for the site plane (8,966 trials), shift DOI along the parking orbit when PDI would otherwise need more than 60 s (2,794 trials), and move PDI ignition P63-style for the site's downrange (largest shift 60 s). All three predict from the trial's TRUE state with the undispersed descent model — there is no lunar-orbit navigation error — so the landing miss from Site 2 (median 1.7 km, 95th percentile 4.5 km, max 9.8 km over 8,774 landings) measures targeting precision. It is tighter than Apollo 11's own touchdown ~6.4 km west of its target, which the crew flew manually past a boulder field. The calibrated mascon long-landing proxy (MASCON_DOWNRANGE_*, mean 4.0 km long) is recorded per trial as land_downrange_err_m and is NOT added to that miss.
5. Surface operations — coefficients are estimate-grade. The 21.6-hour surface stay carries three SOURCED discrete failure modes (ENABLE_DESCENT_FAILURE_MODES): an unrecoverable LM electrical/switchgear fault (~0.85%/mission, anchored to Apollo 11's snapped ascent-engine arming breaker), a touchdown tip-over beyond the ~12° stability limit (~0.5%/landing; Apollo 15 landed at ~11°), and a terminal EVA suit/PLSS failure (~0.1%/mission, from 0 in 28 program man-EVAs). On top of these, ENABLE_SURFACE_OPS (default ON) now flies a per-suit metabolic PLSS consumables ledger over the 2h31m EVA in which FEEDWATER is the thermal margin (MSC-00171 §10.0): distinct CDR/LMP workloads with per-suit metabolic + EVA-duration dispersion, an external lunar thermal load and a cumulative dust-cooling term, reproducing Apollo 11's flown margins at the nominal — so a high-metabolic / extended-EVA tail can breach the 30-min red line into a survivable EVA abort, and if the OPS backup also fails, a per-suit (or both-suits) fatality. RESIDUAL: the dust/thermal/OPS coefficients are estimate-grade (≈ non-binding at Apollo 11's single short EVA — dust minimal, cooling always adequate), the EVA is a lumped metabolic drawdown not a per-task timeline, and the surface clock is held at 21.6 h (a small true-stay refinement is not applied — the open-loop return is hypersensitive to upstream timing).
6. Service-module systems failure (the Apollo 13 mode) — sourcing estimates. A catastrophic CSM systems failure (cryo-tank / fuel-cell, the Apollo 13 class) is drawn per mission at PROB_SM_CATASTROPHIC = 1/15 — the SOURCED rate of 1 such event in 15 crewed CSM flights — striking at a uniformly-drawn fraction of the timeline. It is the single largest crew-risk family in the run (5.8% of trials). The CONSEQUENCE depends on where it lands, across four phases with LM-attached phases getting the lifeboat: translunar p_survive ~0.90 and lunar-orbit ~0.85 (LM still attached — the demonstrated Apollo 13 abort), surface ~0.40 (emergency liftoff to a failing CSM), trans-Earth ~0.10 (post-LM-jettison, no lifeboat — CM batteries last hours, not days). Still ESTIMATED: these per-phase survival values and the catastrophic-vs-recoverable split are unanchored judgment, the strike timing is uniform (not duty-cycle-weighted), and the three RECOVERABLE Apollo SM anomalies (Apollo 15 SPS switch, 16 TVC, Skylab 3 RCS quad leaks) are not separately modeled — only the catastrophic class.
7. Midcourse basis is full 3-DOF; the deadband + residual are estimates. The outbound MCC uses the full 3-DOF MSFN/RTCC corrector (ENABLE_MSFN_NAV, default ON): each translunar slot solves the ENTIRE velocity vector — INCLUDING the radial axis an along-track + cross-track basis omits — to drive the fixed-epoch close-approach point (B-plane AND arrival epoch jointly) onto the package's plan, on a Vonbun-1966-class tracking estimate, executed on truth with the as-flown RCS/SPS-branched residual (0.03 / 0.076 m/s per axis, MSC-00171 Table 8.6-II). The remaining approximations are the estimated ~50 km close-approach deadband (~±21 s timeline drift), the ~10 m/s MCC-1 defer-to-better-tracking threshold, and the honest abstraction that a burn's outcome is seen only at the next tracking slot, not continuously. The package itself models no MCCs: the nominal flies none (MCC-2 was 6.4 m/s as flown). With ENABLE_TLC_IMPACT_RECOVERY on, an impact-class arrival (perilune below −1,000 km before the first correction) is left to the MCC chain instead of being declared a sphere-of-influence miss; the far-miss screen and the post-MCC verdict stay. The screen acts only where perilune falls inside the 3-day translunar coast window, which on some launch dates ends before perilune; no trial here missed the sphere of influence.
8. Moon model & lunar gravity — mascons are a proxy. The run uses a real July-1969 lunar ephemeris — the JPL DE440 numerical lunar ephemeris (ENABLE_JPL_EPHEMERIS, read from the DE440 SPICE kernel and evaluated on barycentric dynamical time with the 1969 ΔT offset) — with Earth rotation anchored to the real launch-epoch GMST, so the return plane and splashdown reflect the true sky; the package was solved on the same conventions (precession of date evaluated exactly at every call). The Moon-fixed frame uses the IAU-2009 lunar orientation (ENABLE_LUNAR_LIBRATION): the true spin-pole + prime-meridian W with the physical-libration terms, so the reported landing point is true selenographic. Lunar gravity is the real GRAIL GRGM1200A field truncated to degree 8 (ENABLE_LUNAR_SH_FIELD), so the parking orbit evolves physically (~5–20 km/day, the documented Apollo behavior). Honest residual: localized maria mascons live at degree ≥50 and are NOT captured by the degree-8 field; their effect on the landing is the calibrated proxy described under the landing item, not integrated gravity.
9. Entry & splashdown — no atmospheric dispersion; the recovery zone is screened. Entry flies CLOSED-LOOP guidance by default (ENABLE_SKIP_ENTRY_GUIDANCE): a g-aware numerical predictor-corrector picks the bank angle from full trajectory predictions (penalty above 7 g, hard guard at 9.5 g — under Apollo's 10 g guidance limit and the 12 g structural bound), with crossrange via deadband bank reversals. ENABLE_APOLLO_ENTRY_GUIDANCE (default ON) flies Apollo constant-drag energy management (TN D-6725) — first dip lift-up to the physical entry-FPA floor, then a constant-drag plateau bleeds energy without skipping — on the sourced US Standard Atmosphere 1976 (ENABLE_USSA76_ENTRY). Peak g is 6.42 g at the nominal / 6.32 g fleet median, matching Apollo's ~6.5 g. Each trial aims at a recovery zone placed 2,784 km (Apollo's EI-to-splash design range) along its OWN entry ground track — or, with ENABLE_WATER_ZONE_REAIM on, when that point is on land or within 25 km of it, at the nearest water point along the same track (1,800–3,800 km) that the entry guidance's own prediction reaches within 10 km and at most 1 g above its design-range peak (1 trial here, it splashed in water); the nominal lands ~3 km from its zone and the fleet's guidance miss is ~3.2 km median (Apollo 11 splashed ~3 km from its aim point). The tail: 3 returns splashed more than 25 km out (max ~80 km), all of them scan-fallback TEIs, entering shallow at -6.12° to -6.15°. Splashdown is FLOWN, not geometric (ENABLE_APOLLO_ELS): a two-phase parachute descent (2 drogues → ~56 m/s, 3 mains → ~9.8 m/s), wind drift, CM flotation (Apollo 11 was stable-2), and an Apollo-faithful parachute-failure mode. The nominal enters at -6.50° (package aim −6.495°, Apollo −6.48°). REMAINING residuals: the absence of winds and per-trial density dispersion, and estimate-grade parachute/flotation rates. The package's recovery target is (13.39°N, 169.26°W); its downrange is a placeholder and its zone crossrange is screened (ill-posed with perigee and time of flight pinned), so the aimed zones sit a median 73 km from it (15% more than 100 km). The nominal splashes at (13.5°N, 169.9°W), ~80 km from Apollo 11's actual splashdown.
