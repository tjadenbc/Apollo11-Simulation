Apollo 11 - Stage-by-Stage Realism Audit

A fidelity-first accounting of how faithfully the simulation re-flies Apollo 11 — pad to splashdown as one continuous trajectory, mission phase by phase. This run is the from-scratch reconstruction: the launch azimuth, trans-lunar-injection preset, descent-orbit timing, and recovery zone are all derived from geometry and physics with no Apollo flight data, and the entry flies the sourced US Standard Atmosphere 1976. This grades faithfulness to the 1969 as-flown mission, not the success probability — the rate is an output; realism is the target.

What changed in this run

From-scratch nominal + USSA-76 entry atmosphere

The nominal is reconstructed with no flown data (azimuth, preset, DOI timing, recovery zone all derived), and the entry uses the sourced USSA-76 profile in place of the legacy exponential — taking peak entry deceleration from the exponential's ~7.1 g nominal down to 6.38 g nominal / 6.42 g fleet median, dead on Apollo's 6.5 g and out of the red on the cross-check. Mission (83.6%) and crew (94.2%) are statistically identical to the flight-anchored definitive — the reconstruction and the atmosphere fix are outcome-neutral.

Launch-window robustness

The reconstruction holds across the window

The definitive nominal is derived from scratch for the on-time (July 16) opportunity, but the same first-principles process reproduces Apollo's physics at the program's backup opportunities and at the flight-anchored azimuth — the fidelity is not specific to one launch time or site. A deliberately adverse off-window date (July 23) is included as a stress test.

Across the 4 matched seed-37 10,000-trial fleets the mission-success rate spans just 83.54–83.83% (a 0.29-point range, every 95% CI overlapping): the reconstructed success rate is a property of the vehicle and design, not of the specific launch day, site, or azimuth. Faithfulness guardrail

1969 as-flown — no forward contamination

Nothing post-Apollo-11 may contaminate the simulation via the sibling Artemis I work. Every port is screened by a nature-vs-artifact test — the sim behaves as the real vehicle and crew did wherever 1969 physics and data allow, diverging only where the record genuinely runs out.

Nature ports freely

Gravity fields, ephemerides, time standards, and atmosphere physics describe the same universe Apollo flew through — the best available model wins regardless of publication date. The entry now uses the same sourced USSA-76 profile as the ascent; where 1969-specific data exists (the AS-506 launch-day sounding) it is preferred over a generic standard.

Artifact never ports

Vehicle constants, guidance and navigation laws, dispersion magnitudes, and failure rates must be Apollo-as-flown, sourced to Apollo-era documentation (GSOP / Colossus, TN D-6725, the AS-506 flight-evaluation report, the mission reports). Ascent stays IGM; entry is Apollo's own — not Orion PredGuid.
